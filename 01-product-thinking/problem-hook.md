# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** Routelogic
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** RouteLogic's differentiation will become a liability rather than an advantage. Doing nothing means ceding the position of “the fast tool” permanently, and then competing on feature breadth alone against a rival who is catching up.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The telling sign is the order of operations: they decide outside the product and enter data inside it later. That is why usage is declining. The tool has moved from being where work happens to being where work is reported.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** We must solve declining adoption, customer churn, and competitive displacement by addressing the daily frustration frontline coordinators experience when critical operational tasks are buried beneath unnecessary complexity.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** Value proposition

The new value: RouteLogic Velocity makes the product fast for the people running the fleet today, and keeps every enterprise capability intact for the people who need it. In practice:

A dispatcher-first default experience. The home view is built around live fleet status, exceptions, and the three or four actions that make up most real-time work (reassign, reroute, notify, confirm). Everything else gets out of the way.
Progressive disclosure for advanced capability. Compliance, financial reporting, and specialized workflows move behind role-based views and an on-demand layer. Administrators and executives keep full power, and dispatchers stop paying a tax for features they never touch.
Evidence-based pruning. Each feature is audited against usage data and research, then sorted into four buckets: core (keep front and center), role-specific (show only to the right roles), advanced (on demand), or legacy (retire or sunset).
A speed commitment we can measure. For example: time to complete a reassignment, clicks to the live fleet view, and the share of dispatch decisions made inside the product versus outside it.

Why launch now:

A live threat with a deadline. The competitor trial is under way and renewal timing is the forcing function. Every month of delay makes a defection harder to reverse.
The gap widens with time. Each new feature we ship under the current model adds more noise, so waiting makes the fix more expensive.
The board has named the risk. There is organizational will now to say no to features, which is rare and usually disappears once the immediate pressure fades.
It protects the enterprise franchise rather than abandoning it. Velocity is framed as keeping the C-suite’s powerful operating system while making it usable, so we avoid a false choice between simple and capable.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** Yes, this is a high-stakes strategic threat, not a minor inconvenience.

Evidence from the brief:

Usage is declining among the primary operational users.
The largest enterprise customer is actively trialing a competitor.
The board is concerned that complexity is hurting the product's core purpose.
The issue impacts adoption, retention, and competitive positioning simultaneously.

If the problem were merely a few users complaining about a difficult workflow, it would be a usability improvement project. Instead, RouteLogic is facing a situation where its core value proposition ("make fleet operations more efficient") is being questioned by customers.
